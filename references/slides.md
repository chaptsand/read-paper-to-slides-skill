# Slides

## Calibrate to the user and select material

Use established audience, structure, language, page or time limits, and content preferences. When a reference deck is supplied for its presentation style, inspect its pages as images to understand the materials selected, what the speaker explains, and how the talk advances. Extracted text alone cannot establish this pacing.

Build around the research problem, necessary mechanism, evaluation, and supported conclusion within the user's chosen structure. Select from the verified reading record; its full coverage does not determine slide coverage. Give the central ideas and difficult steps enough explanation while keeping supporting details proportionate.

If a plan is requested, propose a content map with relevant source anchors. If full slide content is requested, deliver the complete content without requiring another outline approval. Use a page title, visible material, source, and optional notes as helpful fields, rather than a compulsory form.

## Choose material that carries the explanation

Prefer meaningful source passages or faithful Chinese translations for motivation, definitions, and author reasoning. Preserve causal links, qualifications, terminology, and relevant citations. Select and shorten passages at coherent boundaries; distinguish translated or edited excerpts from new explanatory prose.

Use original architecture figures, tables, formulas, and pseudocode where they communicate the content. Keep labels, axes, units, captions, and essential footnotes intact or explain deliberate simplifications. A slide may center on a figure alone, a substantive paragraph, or several related pieces of evidence. Choose density according to the material and how it will be explained.

Separate visible content from speaker explanation and production instructions. Let oral guidance direct attention within rich source material. Include only the notes needed for understanding or handoff; a fixed set of bullets, summary sentences, or source disclaimers is unnecessary.

## Make the mechanism followable

Introduce unfamiliar objects and show a system overview before detailed operations. Revisit an original figure with a highlighted or enlarged region when it supports the next explanation. Keep the overall relationships recognizable; each revisit should advance understanding.

For a central algorithm, follow its state, choice, generated object, execution, returned signal, and update. Successive highlights of the same pseudocode can reveal these steps. A running example can connect an abstract rule to a concrete operation. Clearly identify teaching examples and proposed schematics.

Add prerequisites when they unlock a specific step. An intuitive situation, compact formula, or small numerical table may suffice; connect it back to the paper's objects. Preserve original equation symbols and explain what the calculation controls.

Progressive views count toward the requested slide limit. Allocate them deliberately, alongside the other essential material, without prescribing a fixed number of independent topics or pages.

## Present experiments and conclusions

Establish the task, metric meaning, and comparison conditions needed to interpret the results. Use original evidence and guide attention to the relevant cells or regions. Related tables, figures, and author analysis can share a page when they answer the same research question.

Check numerical values together with headers, averaging units, metric direction, and qualifications. Keep performance, execution success, efficiency, and scientific interpretation distinct. Preserve exceptions that change the conclusion.

Conclude with the author's supported findings and limitations. Include presenter discussion or extensions when they serve the requested talk; keep their status clear.

## Review and hand off

Identify the active deck by its content and actual page count. Inspect relevant pages visually and check substantive claims against the paper. Prioritize mistakes in object roles, objectives, metrics, comparison scope, and numerical interpretation.

For light revisions, preserve correct wording, order, and visuals. Retain purposeful progressive views. Give corrections at actual page numbers with replacement wording and a brief reason, distinguishing factual corrections from optional presentation changes.

For a requested editable deck, inspect all rendered final pages for legibility, overflow, figure fidelity, and equation rendering. For a content-only request, deliver the content and source anchors.

Record the approved deck version and actual page sequence for subsequent scripts. Keep source notes and resolved reading questions available for Q&A; update affected script passages when the deck changes.
